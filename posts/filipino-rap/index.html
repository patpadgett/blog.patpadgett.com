Filipino rap: yes, the words you don't understand count too

Francis M, Andrew E., Gloc-9, and FlipTop give you different ways into Filipino hip-hop. Keep the original audio handy, folks. The translation can't do everything.

If you hear a rapper switch languages halfway through a verse, don't sit there waiting for them to pick one. They already picked the words they wanted, and you and I get to figure out why.

Taglish[1]Ref. 1Taglish en.wikipedia.org is the mixing of Tagalog[2]Ref. 2Tagalog en.wikipedia.org and English. That gives Filipino rappers choices that an English-only transcription can hide completely. A phrase changes rhythm, gets more formal, or suddenly sounds like it's meant for somebody else. Sometimes you hear the switch happen. Sometimes people are just talking the way they talk, and I'm the one making a big production out of it.

An English phrase inside Tagalog might sound ordinary. Or official, intimate, boastful, ridiculous. The switch doesn't come with a permanent emotional label attached. The speaker and the audience supply that, and recognizing a word doesn't mean I get to overrule them. Though it's tempting when I finally recognize something.

As a producer, I want to hear the mechanics. Different words give you different consonant clusters to work with, a borrowed phrase might squeeze an idea into less space, while another word gives you a vowel you can stretch. Switching languages can make a rhyme possible that wouldn't exist if the writer kept the whole passage in one language. I can hear that choice while I'm still working out what it means.

The Philippines has many languages. Filipino hip-hop[3]Ref. 3Filipino hip-hop en.wikipedia.org reaches beyond Tagalog and English, and these nationally visible names can't stand in for every regional scene. Calling every record from the country Taglish would get me into trouble pretty quickly.

Try following the language changes in one passage with a reliable explanation, then play the audio again without reading. Does the switch sound like a change in attitude now, rather than just vocabulary?
